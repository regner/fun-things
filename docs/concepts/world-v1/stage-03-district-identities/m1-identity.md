# M1 identity proposal — bright frontage, working backstreets

9 October 2026 · **Identity direction approved with Stage 3.** All names remain placeholders.

[Signal Row board](06-signal-row.png) · [Ironreach board](08-ironreach.png) ·
[Whole-city briefs](README.md) · [Approved M1 location](../stage-02-city-structure/m1-candidate.md)

The first playable area is a compact neighbourhood where the commercial city meets
the people who keep it running. Bright shops and a small public plaza lead into
warmly lit repair courts, with ordinary homes tucked between them. A downtown tower
and a broader parking edge tie it to the rest of Brackett.

**Recommendation: make the commercial/workshop contrast the identity of M1.** Signal
Row supplies people, colour and a recognisable meeting place; Ironreach supplies
driving escapes, open working space and the main car-chain opportunity. Both should
feel like parts of one neighbourhood. Avoid a hard colour boundary at a district line.

## Character through the six-block programme

These are roles and relationships, not a replacement street plan or six final lots.
The selected approximate extent and two-loop requirement carry forward from Stage 2.

**Owner refinement:** blocks and plots must differ in size. The six themes do not
mean six equal rectangles or one building per block. Several small shop/home plots
can face one larger depot or yard. Use a hierarchy of commercial, local and service
streets, with lane counts and widths appropriate to each role. See the
[city-scale refinement](urban-scale.md); exact dimensions remain proposals.

| Programme | Proposed identity and purpose | Read from above |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shops / foot passage | The brightest everyday frontage: small shops, food counters and exaggerated advertising; a useful walking alternative behind them | Unequal roof depths, a clear passage gap and a few cyan/magenta sign bands |

## Where activity gathers

Put the strongest pedestrian activity around shops and the plaza, and the strongest
car-chain opportunity in the repair/parking yard. Smaller encounters can happen
between them. This gives players a reason to move and makes the neighbourhood easier
to understand than giving every street the same crowd and vehicle density.

The existing **64 pedestrians, 32 cars and four players** remain district-wide totals.
These briefs do not assign a second population budget to either named district.
Large painted parking areas can be mostly empty. Later placement must check the
existing **4.1 m explosion radius** and decide where chains should connect or stop.
No exact car allocation or parking spacing is approved here.

Retain two connected driving loops, a clear shop foot passage, the service escape and
two yard exits. Physical dead ends may add local character where turning and return
routes work. The inland M1 edge still needs deliberate authored limits and returning
routes; an arrow pointing into the unbuilt city is not a playable escape.

The repair/stunt-yard role initially means flat-ground manoeuvring space: hard turns,
near misses and evasive runs. This brief does not introduce ramps, jumps, roof access
or elevated driving. Those would require a separate technical and scope decision.

## Colour, buildings and atmosphere

Use the accepted smooth, stylised 3D city and fixed blue-hour/early-night direction.
Put magenta and cyan at selected commercial anchors; shift toward amber work lights
and petrol sheds through the service streets. Homes contribute smaller warm pools,
while the downtown edge offers a cool cyan crown. Keep roads and most roof interiors
quiet enough that people, cars and brief effects remain distinct.

The proposals on the boards are colour relationships, not calibrated lighting keys.
Do not apply a separate sky or time of day to each district. Actual lighting values,
shadow fill and bloom are Stage 5 work. Wet surfaces in the setting image do not
require a weather or rain system.

Build variety first through silhouette: paired shops, a longer depot, stepped housing,
unequal workshops, a rounded hall and one tower edge. Vary setbacks, gaps and roof
depth before adding detail. This addresses the earlier repeated-building concern
without inventing a new architectural style for every lot. The final family count,
height bands and assets remain Stage 6 and Stage 10 decisions.

## Humour and landmark hierarchy

Keep humour local and practical: dubious bargains on the commercial side, confident
repair promises on the working side, and petty notices around the homes. Draft sign
lines are tonal examples, not final brands or a commitment to readable text at the
gameplay camera.

The rounded hall/plaza is the proposed main orientation cue. The amber repair sheds
identify the yard; the cyan tower identifies the downtown edge. These three cues
have different silhouettes and colours. Stage 8 will design and test their actual
forms; approving this brief does not approve three finished landmark models.

## Trade-offs and next owner choice

The recommended balanced version gives the first slice a representative mix of the
city and its gameplay. A more entertainment-heavy version would strengthen the neon
identity but reduce room for useful service space. A workshop-heavy version would
offer clearer driving and chain areas but underrepresent the social/commercial city.
Both alternatives fit the selected location; neither requires new mechanics.

**Current refinement:** the owner supports the individual district identities and
wants differences in district area, block size, plot size, street width and lane
count made explicit. The recommended balanced commercial/workshop identity remains;
its spatial expression must avoid six interchangeable blocks.

The owner accepted this identity direction and its spatial refinement with Stage 3.
Stage 4 now develops the streets and unequal block arrangement; exact dimensions,
placement and gameplay validation remain subsequent work.
