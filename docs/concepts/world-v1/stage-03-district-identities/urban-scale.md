# Different districts need different amounts of space

9 October 2026 · Owner-directed refinement; examples remain concept proposals.

[Same-scale comparison](10-urban-scale.png) · [District briefs](README.md)

The district characters are positively received. The owner explicitly wants varied
district areas, street widths, lane counts and block sizes. These must be visible in
the next city layout, not just described in the district text.

## Four separate scales

- **Building:** an individual house, shop, workshop or warehouse footprint.
- **Plot:** the building plus its garden, parking, loading or working space. Several
  houses can occupy separate plots along one block; one warehouse can occupy most
  of a much larger block.
- **Block:** land enclosed by streets. Shape, depth, length and subdivision vary
  with land use. Foot passages can cross a block without introducing another road.
- **District:** a recognisable area made of different blocks and open spaces. No
  equal-area, equal-block-count or equal-population quota applies to the nine names.

The earlier **64 × 56 m lot** and **81 × 73 m module** are historical M1/area-comparison
references, not universal city tiles. The nine district boards use the same page
format and independently framed motifs; their panel sizes do not compare real areas.

## Proposed distribution

| Area | Block and plot pattern | Street hierarchy to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northpoint | Large campus grounds and sports plots, smaller academic courts within them | Two-lane perimeter access; narrower service access; pedestrian paths across grounds |
| The Crescents | Many small house/garden plots within short, curved and occasionally longer residential blocks | Narrow two-way local streets, a few single-lane one-way access courts, larger connecting streets |
| Terrace Ward | Medium and larger apartment plots, stepped blocks and shared courts; more than one block size | Two-lane neighbourhood streets, narrower access lanes and separate foot links |
| Glassward | A substantial district with unequal tower/podium sites and open forecourts; towers need not consume whole blocks | Selected four-lane avenues, two-lane cross streets and smaller service access |
| Old Quay | Fine-grained, irregular shop/home plots interrupted by larger civic blocks | Mainly two-lane streets; selected one-way lanes where useful; wider approaches only where justified |
| Signal Row | Short shopping blocks beside larger hall/plaza plots, with mixed subdivision behind the frontage | Two-lane commercial streets with generous pedestrian space; a larger connector at selected edges; narrow rear access |
| Broadlot | Large, irregular retail/parking sites with separate small shop plots around them | Four-lane approaches where useful, two-lane site access and simpler parking aisles |
| Ironreach | Small and medium repair plots mixed with larger depot/yard sites | Two-lane working streets, narrower service links and wider turning space at yards |
| East Docks | The longest warehouse/yard blocks; broad loading aprons and substantial space between public junctions | Broad two-lane freight access with generous turns; four lanes only on an approach that warrants them |

These are a mix within each district, not nine uniform templates. A large site does
not automatically need more traffic lanes: two broad freight lanes with turning
space can be more appropriate than four lanes. A busy pedestrian street can have
two traffic lanes and wide sidewalks. Parking and loading bays are additional space,
not extra through lanes.

Keep the already selected large downtown, generous retail parking, campus fields
and long dock sites. Allow their land needs to shape district boundaries; do not
divide the island into nine equal pieces. Preserve the approved broad relationships
while refining those boundaries. The city envelope remains about 1.2 × 0.65 km.

## Road continuity and M1

The ordinary **9 m carriageway plus 4 m sidewalks** remains one reference street
type. The owner now authorizes exploring both narrower streets and wider multi-lane
roads. It is no longer a minimum corridor imposed on every street. Final lane and
sidewalk dimensions, one-way routing, transitions, crossings and traffic behaviour
belong in Stage 4 and later validation; no engine support is claimed here.

Road roles should follow useful routes across district borders, with deliberate
junctions and transitions. Do not change lane count simply because a road crosses a
colour boundary on the map. Avoid a four-lane avenue ending abruptly in a tiny court.

For M1, retain the approximate six-block scope and its content programme, but use
**unequal blocks and multiple plots per block**. Several small shop or housing plots
can sit opposite one larger depot or yard. A modest commercial connector, ordinary
local streets and narrower service links should establish contrast within the slice;
including every city-wide road class is not required. Keep two driving loops, foot
alternatives and two yard exits. Extra road area must be accounted for within the
size study; any material footprint increase returns for review.

## How the comparison board works

The three squares show equal **180 × 180 m illustrative land samples**, not equally
sized districts or final blocks. Every object uses the same drawing scale. Small
houses and plots, medium commercial/workshop sites, and large warehouses/working
yards can therefore be compared directly. Each sample includes more than one street
or site size; panel edges are crop boundaries, not enclosing streets.

Illustration values are deliberately provisional: residential carriageway 7 m,
ordinary commercial street 9 m, four-lane connector 14 m, and broad two-lane freight
street 10 m. They demonstrate a hierarchy rather than approve cross-sections. The
fixed car symbols are 4.2 × 1.8 m throughout. Plot outlines are diagram keys, not
walls; the drawings do not validate clearances, turns, camera visibility or capacity.

Before detailed road-kit work is accepted, the refined city plan should show varied
block outlines inside unequal districts and a legible road hierarchy. It must be
possible to distinguish the residential, commercial and industrial areas even with
their labels and district colours hidden.
