# East Docks asset breakdown

The owner likes the warehouse/crane direction and requests the boardwalk end at
both district boundaries. This set covers [revision 02](09-east-docks-v02.png).
The [central registry](asset-register.md#09-east-docks-assets) owns demand and
progress; this breakdown explains the required reusable designs and shared uses.
Dimensions, individual designs and placed quantities still need refinement.

## Warehouse and crane assemblies

| Required design | Owning brief and coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Long ridge warehouse | [Warehouse kit](../../assets/d09_warehouses.md), `.01`; long roof, wall/loading-door bays and normal industrial trim |
| Wide low warehouse | Same brief, `.02`; broader low assembly using compatible bays and roof parts |
| Loading-face annex | Same brief, `.03`; attached loading/entry component, not a third complete warehouse |
| Larger dock crane | [Crane pair](../../assets/d09_cranes.md), `.01`; base, structure, cab, boom and static cable/hook appearance |
| Smaller dock crane | Same brief, `.02`; related parts with a distinct smaller silhouette |
| Broad roof vent and compact plant enclosure | [Shared roof details](../../assets/city_roof_details.md), `.01–.02`; sparse reuse, not machinery on every roof |

Use shared wall, loading-door, roof and annex interfaces across warehouse lengths.
Normal roller shutters, loading lips, windows, gutters and facade trim belong to
these assemblies. Refine compatible parts before any later production; the image
does not prove module joins. Crane parts may share geometry where compatible;
the unequal pair is not two unrelated detailed machinery projects.

applied artwork, not a separate sign model. Service vehicles and people reuse
existing city tracks; no new ship, cargo inventory or freight machinery system is
required by this concept.

The boardwalk ends in neighbouring Ironreach (08) and Broadlot (07). East Docks
has no boardwalk, coastal promenade or landward boardwalk bypass. Existing trim,
rail and access parts finish the neighbouring terminals. Normal street footways
remain layout context. A selective working-edge safety rail is a supporting use
to confirm, not a replacement continuous promenade railing.

## Supporting uses and deferred surfaces

Short pedestrian bollards, solid service barriers, chain-link panels/posts/gates,
utility cabinets, drain covers, generic rear service doors, public bins, service
dumpsters, selective quay rails, edge ladders, wheel stops and parking-sign
assemblies remain supporting uses to confirm in closer yard concepts. Shared
hardware covers them; parking signs reuse the low panel design. Do not place them
across freight turns, the service spur or ordinary street footways.

Road surfaces and ordinary road/parking/loading markings remain deferred to future
surface tooling. Concrete here is a visual study for working yards, not a road
mesh kit. No modelling, implementation, crane controls, ship handling, interiors
or animation is commissioned. Exact crane/vehicle clearances and fixed-camera
visibility still need later measured checks.
