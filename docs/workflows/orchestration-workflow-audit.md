**Independent workflow audit — Fun Things, 8 October 2026**

The workflow protects important boundaries, but repeatedly turns completion into another coordination task. The largest demonstrated inefficiencies are frequent whole-plan reconciliation, review/retention tails, ambiguous worker roles, and recovery grants that require ROOT to intervene at very small steps. Changing models alone will not address those patterns. Later sessions already contain useful improvements; consolidate them before adding more process.

This is an independent orchestration/communication audit, not a technical verdict on the game or pending workers. References `[E01]` etc. identify short public excerpts with exact session ID, JSONL path, event line and UTC in `evidence.json`.

**Coverage and limits.** I inventoried the supplied 211 local sessions, including archived sessions, and examined workflow communications, relevant tool calls and chronology in 15 selected sessions. Selection spans early and late work: ROOT; first/eighth checkpoints; DOC1/2 and small DOC3; model-policy correction; initial S02; both the redundant S03-R coordinator and actual implementer; S06 review/retention; preceding S05 source/S08 preparation; latest S05/S08 leads and S05 reviewer. This includes successful delivery, useful defect discovery, correction of process mistakes and interrupted partial work. I did not read every large tool payload or unpack historical archives.

Of 211 sessions, **111 are automatic permission guardians**, distinguished by `source.subagent.other=guardian`; the other 100 comprise 80 `vscode` sessions and 20 collaboration-spawn sessions. They are not 211 deliberately staffed workers. Model/effort comes from `turn_context`, not `model_provider`: 64 non-guardian sessions expose Sol6.1 HIGH and 17 MEDIUM at least once; mixed sessions overlap. The sample includes an Astra→Sol lead switch and a successful short Luna HIGH review. No pricing or model-quality conclusion is supportable here.

The working snapshot ends for ROOT at **09:35:21 UTC**; latest workers/reviewer have their own cutoffs in the ledger. Session duration includes waits, and cumulative token usage includes cached repeated context. ROOT records approximately **71.90m input tokens, 70.56m cached input, and 316,628 output tokens**; this is neither unique text volume nor money spent. Its public response records contain 348 assistant messages and 341 `exec` calls. Syntactic nested-call counts include 124 follow-up sends, 36 agent creations and 24 profile listings; loops/dynamic calls can differ from executions. These are workload proxies, not proof of wasted effort.

Authority matters: the **user** required ROOT to remain orchestration-only, isolated worktrees, independent clean-context review, linear integration and periodic plan/profile assessment [E01–E03]. **Tools/system** impose sandbox and approval behavior. The **project skill** specified checkpoint cadence, launch discovery and review/retention rules. **ROOT** added operational reservations, serialized review slots and granular stop/regrant instructions. The audit evaluates the last two rather than treating every convention as a user requirement.

**1. Whole-plan checkpoints generate a documentation treadmill. High impact; recurring, already partly corrected.**

Eight full checkpoints were recorded across 7–8 October. The original skill used approximately three integrated workstreams as its normal trigger; the eighth assignment counted DOC10/11, stopped S02 observation and S08 preparation as three [E34]. Whole-plan checking produces scoped documentation tasks; their completion and subsequent partial evidence produce another whole-plan reconciliation. The user requested periodic accuracy, not this frequency. ROOT explicitly admitted the cost was excessive [E08, E37].

Task definitions do have usable `Needs`, outputs, questions, budgets and done criteria. Completed items are removed with resolving changes, while unproved gates stay open. The problem is that TODO also accumulates historical acceptance, live ownership, operational grants and readiness prose. It grew from **544 lines/4,810 whitespace words to 965/8,375**, while the orchestration skill grew from **189/1,507 to 373/3,187**. Growth alone is not waste, but accepted-main notifications and TODO ownership conflicts show its coordination burden [E36].

**Change:** enforce the accepted 6–8 substantive-workstream/milestone cadence; update affected contracts during ordinary resolving work, create bounded tasks for unrelated drift, and keep live ownership in one compact coordination record. TODO should emphasize outcome, dependency, owner, remaining acceptance and evidence link. Preserve historical facts in dated records. **Tradeoff:** cross-cutting drift may wait longer; cheap dependency/doc checks at each handoff compensate. The first checkpoint genuinely allocated missing proof owners and established a reliable watermark [E15]. Keep that benefit.

**2. Retention creates a second completion loop. High impact; established historical pattern, improving.**

DOC1/2 was substantively accepted at **20:06:29**; storing the review changed HEAD, and exact retention-revision approval arrived at **20:12:21**, 5.9 minutes later [E17–E18]. The first checkpoint and S03-R similarly added report-only revision review after substantive approval [E16, E27]. In S06, the fixed candidate was accepted at **03:07:10**; note storage/readback and an extra unchanged-HEAD acknowledgement continued until **03:13:59** [E31–E33]. These are observed tails, not all attributable to unnecessary work.

Complete diagnostics and original findings matter: S06 exposed both a real boundary bug and an omitted evidence item [E30]. Nevertheless, report→commit→review→note→acknowledgement can expand indefinitely without changing the underlying candidate. Hundreds of artifacts and references become another acceptance surface.

**Change:** freeze the implementation, independently review/fix it, obtain the same reviewer's exact final disposition, then retain one declared package and verify expected-set/hash/readback once. Evidence-only note storage should not automatically create another review. Renew assessment for material candidate/base/evidence changes or a concrete unresolved condition. **Tradeoff:** deterministic retention verification carries more responsibility; disclose missing streams rather than reconstructing them. This is already the amended rule, and S05 source preparation demonstrates unchanged-HEAD retention without renewal [E42–E43].

**3. Role contamination duplicates agents and discovery. High impact; repeated early examples, corrected later.**

An S03-R worker said, “delegate implementation to a Sol6.1 HIGH workspace lead,” because it interpreted ROOT's orchestration-only restriction as its own [E24]. ROOT adopted the newly created implementer directly and parked the redundant coordinator [E25]. DOC3 similarly had a parent/implementer split: the child reported an independently reviewed commit, then the parent commissioned another review [E20–E21]. ROOT-role instructions in a skill read by workers contributed to confusion; public responses establish the interpretation, though model behavior cannot be reduced to one cause.

DOC3's parent explicitly told the child that discovery was already complete and to avoid repeated Paseo calls because of permission interruptions [E19]. This reflects mandated launch discovery plus duplicated role ownership, not a reason to bypass approvals. A newer S08 parent notification was itself rejected by automatic approval as prohibited peer contact [E45], so safe routing also needs explicit identity.

**Change:** separate ROOT, implementer and reviewer contracts; one Sol lead implements each workstream, with necessary bounded specialists and one independent reviewer. Pass launch settings and parent ID as a receipt; discover again only for a new launch or meaningful drift. Use MEDIUM for routine documentation and HIGH for demanding work; keep Astra within the clarified specialist scope [E05]. **Tradeoff:** receipts can age; validate them when conditions change. The focused policy edit's short Luna review is a strong proportionate counterexample [E22].

**4. Stop/regrant granularity makes ROOT the recovery interpreter. High impact; recurring latest sequence.**

S05's supervisor terminated an authenticated editor on a recoverable tool limitation, losing an unsaved child; run02 stopped on an old registration before waiting out its budget; run03 stopped on a stale-process diagnostic; run04 finally reached readiness [E51–E54]. S08 separately stopped after a readiness-expression mistake, discovery-schema error and truncated request transport [E40, E47]. ROOT repeatedly amended grants, split preparation/authoring/validation gates and relayed lessons [E46, E48].

These were changed-condition attempts, not demonstrated identical retries. The inefficiency is repeated renegotiation of recovery boundaries and bespoke supervisory behavior. Exit zero was correctly distinguished from diagnostic acceptance, but one failed phase sometimes prevented an independently meaningful later phase until ROOT explicitly separated them.

**Change:** task cards should declare total attempt/time budget, owned resources, preservation duties, recoverable classes, phase-specific acceptance and escalation triggers. Let the worker resolve ordinary owned-request/supervisor mistakes inside that envelope; escalate new shared resources, access, scope or exhausted budgets. Independently assess failed and successful phases without rewriting historical verdicts. **Tradeoff:** broader recovery authority needs precise boundaries and receipts. Fail-closed identity checks and honest stopped outcomes remain valuable; this finding does not prescribe engine repairs.

**5. Precautionary locks and integration churn constrain parallel work. Medium-high impact; observed friction, avoidability partly uncertain.**

The user challenged both the one-writer interpretation and named-port requirements [E09–E10]. Workers later distinguished a shared-instance lock from independent worktree authoring, and fixed reservations from supported discovery [E41]. The eighth checkpoint was ready at **06:06:59**, but its reviewer was held until **06:18:04**; meanwhile main advanced, requiring reconciliation before review [E35–E36]. Earlier S02 and S03-R also received documentation-only main advances during review.

**Change:** lock the actual shared writer/resource. Record reviewer queues as resource-conflict or capacity decisions, with a release condition; park delivered workers if active-slot capacity is the constraint. Batch unrelated documentation integration around near-ready technical candidates where that reduces repeated rebases. Preserve exact-base review: the same reviewer checks the actual delta when base changes. **Tradeoff:** batches delay some merges, and concurrency may still be capacity-limited. The logs do not prove the review hold was unnecessary; real shared-editor reviews justify serialization. Later S05/S08 parallel launch is the positive direction [E14].

**6. Prompts encode growing procedural history and lose readability. Medium impact; established size/duplication, performance effect uncertain.**

Latest launch prompts measure **10,338 characters for S08 and 10,017 for S05** [E44, E50], before repository guidance and referenced materials. Older DOC1/2 was 3,860 characters. Later text frequently joins words (`acceptedLOCALmain`, `ROOTEXPLICITLY`) and repeats model, role, history, retention, ownership and stop conditions. Whitespace word counts therefore understate prompt size. The skill's worker/reviewer templates also repeat checkpoint policy even for ordinary tasks.

**Change:** use a readable short task card with outcome, exact inputs, scope, owner, acceptance, recovery budget and review/handoff requirements. Link one versioned role contract and one evidence index. Preserve the complete raw user requirements and authoritative grants separately; the reviewer receives them without an implementer verdict. Follow-ups should state the changed fact and its consequence. **Tradeoff:** references introduce navigation; eliminate contradictory copies and make critical boundaries explicit in the card. Prompt length correlates with difficult work here; no causal token/performance claim is established.

**7. Status sometimes replaces authorized action. Medium impact; one direct admission plus recurring low-information updates.**

At **08:28:07**, ROOT reported clean main, no active workers and the next bounded S08 work. At **08:32:54**, the user asked, “So what are you waiting for?” ROOT answered, “Nothing external. I should have started…” [E11–E13]. Multiple sampled workers also repeated frozen/waiting/retention-ready updates without a changed decision.

**Change:** every ROOT status names the action underway, real blocker and next trigger. Start authorized ready work before reporting it; where approval is genuinely missing, identify the exact missing authority. Use asynchronous completion notifications; during active turns keep required updates concise and meaningful. **Tradeoff:** reduced narration may feel less continuous, so retain milestone updates and user-requested status. ROOT generally did use notifications rather than heavy polling; preserve that, and the safe parking of pending S08 work when the user changed priority [E49].

**Lean end-to-end workflow.** ROOT selects a bounded ready task and records dependencies/ownership once. One Sol lead receives a task card and launch receipt, implements within its recovery envelope, validates meaningful acceptance and updates affected docs/TODO. It freezes a candidate; one clean-context reviewer assesses raw requirements and artifacts. The worker fixes actionable findings, and the same reviewer disposes the exact final delta. One evidence package is verified and handed off. ROOT checks readiness, quiescence and topology, integrates linearly, verifies and archives. Cheap handoff checks run routinely; full plan/profile reviews run at the accepted milestone cadence. Targeted Astra specialists remain optional and justified by the actual question.

Concise formats:

- **Task:** `Outcome: … | Owner: … | Needs: accepted evidence … | Scope/exclusions: … | Done: observable checks … | Budget/stop: … | Remaining external gate: …`
- **Worker prompt:** `Implement task T in workspace W, base B. Own paths P. Read role contract R and raw requirements Q. Deliver criteria C within recovery budget K. Use one independent reviewer; no integration authority.`
- **Follow-up:** `Main advanced B→B2; delta affects docs/TODO only. Finish current measurement, inspect delta at saved boundary, rebase, obtain same-reviewer final disposition.`
- **Status:** `Delivered X. Running Y. Blocked Z because resource/input A is unavailable. Next trigger: B; owner C.`
- **Handoff:** `HEAD/base; scope and TODO result; reviewer/verdict/exact revision; check outcomes and limits; evidence index/readback; clean saved/quiescent state; remaining gate.`

**Three immediate changes:** enforce the amended checkpoint cadence; remove automatic unchanged-HEAD retention acknowledgements; separate ROOT/worker/reviewer contracts and issue readable task cards with recovery budgets. These preserve the strongest controls: clean-context independence caught substantive S02, S03-R and S06 defects [E26, E28–E30], and partial evidence remained distinct from product acceptance [E55].

**Staged follow-up:** over the next 6–8 substantive workstreams, record time to first useful artifact, substantive acceptance→integration tail, coordination-only agents, follow-up count, prompt characters, reviewer queue time/reason, retention failures and user nudges required to start ready work. Compare like task categories, not an aggregate speed score. Then automate stable launch/ownership receipts and evidence verification; consolidate a shared operational handoff only after recurring needs are demonstrated. Revisit profiles using these observations, without manufacturing specialist launches. Improvement means shorter tails and fewer coordination turns while defect discovery, truthful gates and retention completeness remain intact; no numerical savings are promised from this sample.
