Status: bounded foundation spike, 8 October 2026. This record proposes an M1 audio
route and reports a technical fixture; it does not select production sounds or
claim a listening, Deck, sustained-load, or integrated-gameplay pass. The interactive
Godot editor was unavailable, so the small format-3 scene and bus resource were first
written directly, then normalized through an isolated addon-free headless editor on
pinned Godot `4.8.dev7.official.c971f93e7`. That editor save assigned resource,
dependency, and node identities. A subsequent no-preview editor save/reload was
byte-identical. There was no open editor state to synchronize.

## Question and constraints

How should M1 deliver weapon/hit/explosion, engine/tires, footsteps, UI, and
music/ambience while preserving readable priorities, local Master/Music/SFX
settings, and source/license records? Audio must remain bounded even though the
owner removed the cap on **visual** explosion presentations. The expected world can
contain 32 cars, 64 pedestrians, four players, and chain bursts. The gameplay
camera is fixed north-up, vertically down, perspective, 47 m high with 42° FOV.

This spike tests the riskiest local boundary: 32 spatial engine emitters plus 24
simultaneous explosion requests and an immediate 12-shot SMG burst. It deliberately
does not make dropped audio events into dropped gameplay or visual events.

## Sources

Godot sources were checked against the APIs present in the pinned build:

- [Audio buses](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/audio/audio_buses.html)
  describe named routing, sends, and effects; the fixture uses a local bus layout
  rather than changing the project default.
- [`AudioStreamPlayer3D`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audiostreamplayer3d.html)
  documents distance attenuation, `max_distance`, listener-relative panning, and
  `max_polyphony`. A player's polyphony limit is not a global category budget.
- [`AudioListener3D`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audiolistener3d.html)
  establishes the explicit active-listener override used by the fixture.
- [`AudioStreamRandomizer`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audiostreamrandomizer.html)
  provides weighted stream selection plus bounded pitch/volume variation.
- [`AudioEffectLimiter`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audioeffectlimiter.html)
  and [`AudioEffectCompressor`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_audioeffectcompressor.html)
  document safety limiting and sidechain-capable dynamics. The fixture uses a
  Master limiter and explicit short category ducking; production can compare a
  compressor sidechain using real assets.
- [`ConfigFile`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_configfile.html)
  supports section/key local configuration and `user://` persistence.
- Audiokinetic's [voice-management guidance](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/public-library/2024.1.8_8893/?source=Help&id=managing_voices)
  distinguishes physical/virtual voices and priority behavior. It supports making
  voice selection an explicit policy rather than assuming every requested sound
  should reach the mixer.
- [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) defines the waiver
  used for this spike's generated placeholders. [Freesound's licensing FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/#licenses-0)
  is a reminder that library files can have different licenses and attribution
  duties; “free download” is not sufficient provenance.

## Options and decision proposal

### Voice policy

| Option | Fit | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Play every requested source | Minimal code; every event is audible | 32 engines plus dense chains and weapons compete unpredictably, increase mixer work, and can become unintelligible. `max_polyphony` on each emitter does not bound the category. Rejected for M1. |
| Fixed category pools with priority | Predictable work; nearest-N continuous engines; short event pools; explicit drops; simple telemetry | A cap can suppress a useful sound and needs tuning. Pool sizes reserve voices even when another category is idle. **Recommended M1 baseline.** |
| One global weighted allocator with virtualization/stealing | Can trade idle capacity among categories and preserve high-priority tails | More state, cross-category policy, stealing artifacts, and lifecycle complexity than this spike justifies. Reconsider only after integrated measurements show fixed pools are too rigid. |

Proposed starting caps are eight nearest engine loops, eight simultaneous explosion
sounds, and six weapon shots. These are **prototype values, not ratified production
limits**. Explosion audio requests beyond eight are dropped while all gameplay
outcomes and uncapped visual presentations continue. Within the explosion category,
production should prefer closer, locally caused, or newly important events and avoid
restarting identical tails. A -8 dB engine / -4 dB weapon duck for 280 ms makes a
blast readable; a Master limiter at -1 dB is a final safety layer, not a loudness fix.

### Listener placement

1. A listener on the 47 m camera preserves obvious camera-relative panning but also
   adds 47 m vertical distance to every ground source, forcing unnatural attenuation
   compensation.
2. A listener following actor position and actor facing preserves local distance but
   would rotate the stereo field when mouse-facing changes under a fixed north-up
   camera.
3. **Recommended:** listener translation follows the controlled character/car near
   ground level, while orientation remains the fixed north-up/down camera orientation.
   This keeps ground-relative distances and stable screen-left/screen-right panning.

The fixture implements option 3 at `(0, 1, 0)`, oriented straight down like the
camera. The camera itself remains at 47 m / 42°. A future camera transition between
foot and car should move the listener anchor without changing north-up orientation.
Final attenuation curves and speaker/headphone panning require human listening.

### Content, pitch, layers, and persistence

- Engine pitch maps measured speed 0–30 m/s to 0.75–1.55, clamped. Production should
  smooth RPM/speed input, preserve a stable loop phase, add tire/skid as a separate
  state-driven layer, and stop owned loops on despawn/reset/teardown.
- Explosion and SMG pools use `AudioStreamRandomizer` (three and two generated
  variants respectively) with narrow pitch/volume ranges. Randomization must not
  become gameplay randomness or replay on prediction correction/duplicate events.
- Music and ambience are separate non-spatial players. `Ambience -> Music -> Master`
  lets the Music preference cover both for this fixture. Production may expose an
  ambience setting only if product scope changes; current M1 promises Master/Music/SFX.
- `S14Settings` owns schema 1 and persists linear Master/Music/SFX levels plus mute
  values through `ConfigFile` under `user://`. It clamps loaded levels, accepts mute
  values only when they are actual booleans, and otherwise retains safe defaults.
  Production `LocalSettings` should own this state process-wide; it is never
  replicated and a shared match must not pause while settings change.

## Prototype

[`prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/audio_test.tscn`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/audio_test.tscn) is
saved composition containing:

- the ground-position/fixed-camera-orientation listener rig and 47 m / 42° camera;
- 32 authored `AudioStreamPlayer3D` engine emitters;
- saved pools of eight explosion and six SMG players, each with `max_polyphony = 1`;
- independent music and ambience players and a small explanatory overlay;
- [`S14VoiceManager`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/s14_voice_manager.gd), which selects
  nearest-N engines, clamps speed pitch, accounts accepted/dropped event audio,
  applies short explosion ducking, and restores buses on teardown;
- a fixture-local [`AudioBusLayout`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/bus_layout.tres) with
  Master/Music/SFX/Engines/Weapons/Explosions/UI/Ambience routing and limiter; the
  repository's `default_bus_layout.tres` is intentionally unchanged; and
- [`S14Settings`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/s14_settings.gd), tested via its public
  save/load/apply/snapshot APIs, including malformed string/numeric mute values.

The normalized scene has one stable scene UID, UIDs on all ten external resources,
and stable IDs on all 59 nodes. The bus resource has its own UID. A clean isolated
editor roundtrip preserved both resources byte-for-byte while retaining 32 engine,
eight explosion, and six weapon bus assignments. See
[`resource-roundtrip.json`](s14-evidence/resource-roundtrip.json).

Eight mono 44.1 kHz, 16-bit PCM WAV placeholders are deterministically generated by
[`generate_audio.py`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/generate_audio.py). They contain only
mathematical tones and seeded noise. [`audio/LICENSE.md`](../../prototypes/s14/tests/fixtures/s14/audio/LICENSE.md)
records creator, method, date, and CC0 waiver. WAVs and Godot `.import` settings are
committed. These are technical placeholders, not production selections.

[`prototypes/s14/tools/s14/run.py`](../../prototypes/s14/tools/s14/run.py) obtains one committed fixture inventory
from `git ls-files` and uses that same list for staging and SHA-256 source binding.
Thus committed `.import` files are included while ignored/untracked bytecode and other
workstation artifacts are excluded. It stages that inventory into a fresh external,
addon-free project; validates the exact engine; imports; runs three headless Dummy
and three capped-60 windowed WASAPI cases; samples concurrent Godot-process count and
workstation CPU before each case; retains logs and structured receipts; and rejects
unexpected engine diagnostics or semantic cap/settings failures. PASS requires exact
caps and instantaneous peaks of 8 engines, 8 explosions, and 6 weapons, plus malformed
settings retaining defaults. Offline unit tests reject under-active peaks, wrong caps,
either half of the historical diagnostic pair, different leak counts, the exact pair
on WASAPI, divergent staged/manifest inventories, and missing saved identities; they
also check WAV format.

present. The short windowed run includes startup and a 74.303 ms worst sample. More
importantly, the pinned `Performance` API exposes audio output latency but no
per-bus/audio-mix CPU monitor. Whole-process time cannot be attributed to audio and
is recorded only as context; this spike makes no audio CPU-budget claim. A quiet,
longer integrated measurement must use external profiling or an engine/audio-driver
instrument with a defined boundary.

All final run09 logs were clean after the fixture began freeing its scene before the
process exits. An earlier committed-source run02 observed exactly `23 ObjectDB
instances` and `7 resources still in use` during a Dummy-driver run; its log does
not establish that audio or the Dummy driver caused those generic engine diagnostics.
The runner retains a narrow historical exception only when both complete lines match
those exact counts and the receipt says Dummy. Either exact line alone, any different
count, a partial match, or the complete pair under WASAPI fails; regression tests
cover those boundaries. No
process owned by another lane was stopped.

## What is proven and what is not

Proven in this fixture:

- a saved 32-engine layout activates only the nearest eight;
- a 24-request explosion storm and 12-request SMG burst stay at category caps with
  exact audio-only drop accounting;
- generated randomizer resources, bus routing, limiter, bounded duck/restore, and
  speed-pitch mapping load and run on the pinned engine;
- Master/Music/SFX levels and mutes survive an exact `ConfigFile` roundtrip and apply
  to the named buses, while malformed mute types retain safe defaults;
- normalized scene, dependency, bus, and node identities survive a clean editor
  save/reload roundtrip; and
- the same runtime assertions pass with Dummy and actual Windows WASAPI output paths.

Not proven:

- human readability, pleasantness, clipping/distortion, useful spatial panning,
  attenuation at gameplay-camera positions, seamless looping, music taste, or mix;
- actual production weapon/hit/explosion/engine/tire/footstep/UI/music/ambience assets;
- integrated cars/pedestrians, predicted/replayed actions, EventId deduplication,
  reset/leave/rejoin, focus/suspend, or scene transition lifecycle;
- sustained audio CPU, memory, 10-minute load, four-player/far-view behavior, Deck,
  Linux audio, exports, headphones/speakers, accessibility, or final cap tuning; and
- production-safe interrupted-write recovery or settings migration beyond schema 1.

No human listening was possible in this agent session. A successful WASAPI process
only proves that the driver path ran; it does not establish that anyone heard or
approved the mix.

## Owner listening checklist

Run the saved scene windowed at volume that is known safe, then record hardware,
output mode, and concrete findings:

1. At low and normal volume, are the SMG and blast distinct without hard clipping,
   harsh limiter pumping, or speaker/headphone discomfort?
2. Does the blast become prominent while engines/weapons duck, then recover without
   an obvious volume jump around 280 ms?
3. Is east/west panning stable under the north-up view, and do near engines read as
   closer than far engines without the 47 m camera height weakening everything?
4. Can individual engine motion/speed be understood under gunfire, and are six/eight
   event caps acceptable during dense action rather than sounding broken?
5. Do music and ambience support the Petrol & Coral tone without masking gameplay;
   do Master/Music/SFX sliders and each mute behave immediately after restart?

Stop and lower gain if the synthetic noise is uncomfortable. Replace placeholders
before judging final loudness, timbre, or style.

## M1 production requirements

1. Keep `LocalSettings` as the sole process-local writer for schema-versioned
   Master/Music/SFX levels and mutes; validate malformed values, define migration and
   interrupted-write behavior, and test restart plus read-only/failure paths.
2. Author the production bus layout and saved reusable emitter/player scenes; preserve
   a Master true-peak safety margin, measure gain staging with final assets, and do
   not use limiting to hide an over-loud mix.
3. Implement one presentation-owned voice service with explicit category caps,
   priority and drop/steal telemetry. Start from nearest eight engines / eight blasts /
   six weapons, then tune by integrated listening and measured target costs.
4. Separate uncapped visual explosion presentation and authoritative damage/chains
   from bounded audio. Audio drops, virtualization, replay suppression, or off-camera
   culling must never discard gameplay outcomes.
5. Drive each car's loop from smoothed authoritative/predicted display speed, add
   distinct tire/skid state, and stop/rebind loops on seat/life/entity/reset/teardown
   revision changes without duplicate prediction sounds.
6. Deduplicate one-shot audio by stable committed event identity. Preserve suitable
   impact/explosion tails on emitter removal while preventing late packets, replay,
   resync, and hydration from replaying historical sounds.
7. Implement footstep surface/state cadence, UI focus/confirm/error cues, weapon/hit
   families, and music/ambience layering as presentation consumers, not rule owners.
8. For every production audio file, record asset ID, original creator/source URL,
   exact license and attribution text, source file/hash, edits/tool/version/settings,
   runtime export/import settings, loop points, loudness review, buses, and consumers.
   Reject unknown rights. A tool's open-source license alone does not establish the
   license of third-party samples or generated output.
9. Prefer commissioned/self-made material or clearly documented CC0 libraries for
   low-friction licensing. If using Freesound or another mixed-license library, verify
   each selected file rather than the site name. Generated SFX tools such as
   sfxr/ChipTone/jsfxr still need tool/version/preset provenance and an explicit
   project license decision for their outputs.
10. Validate final audio with human headphone and speaker passes, Windows and Linux
    exports, Deck when available, 32 cars/64 pedestrians/four players, distant views,
    chain storms, reset/leave/rejoin, focus/suspend, and sustained external audio CPU/
    memory profiling. Record quiet and contended runs separately.
