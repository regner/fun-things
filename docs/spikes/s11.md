# S11 — population replication, bandwidth and spawning foundation

9 October 2026. Decision proposal and bounded prototype; not production population,
AI, combat, visual-smoothness or M1 acceptance. The exact measured source is bound
in [retained evidence](s11-evidence/README.md). The pinned engine remains
`4.8.dev7.official.c971f93e7`; ENet is the only current transport.

## Question and decision

Can one listen server replicate the simultaneous M1 caps — 64 pedestrians, 32 live
cars, four players, 16 rockets, 16 retained wrecks and 16 retained dead pedestrians —
to three clients within the four bandwidth budgets and 1 MiB join budget, while
keeping durable lifecycle separate from replaceable motion?

**Proposal:** use the existing single Replication writer with an explicit packed
movement codec and reliable state stream. Publish compact complete rows for the
entities selected by a bounded rate schedule; do not introduce per-client delta
state in M1 unless later real-state measurements require it. Keep generation/life
transitions and tombstones reliable, and reject movement until their dependencies
are installed. Start by replicating the whole district to every admitted client.
At this scale, measured traffic has enough margin that interest-management state and
its admission edge cases cost more than they save.

The prototype establishes bandwidth feasibility for a synthetic fixed schema. It
also finds an unresolved presentation risk: a 200 ms interpolation buffer on the
ordered-unreliable stream still extrapolated a median 11.69% of sampled entity frames
under normal delivery and 19.53% under adverse delivery in a heavily contended run.
No rendered entities were observed. Therefore S11 does **not** claim that remote
entities look smooth. Production must tune rate/buffer/freeze behavior with actual
actors in drawable processes.

## Research

- Godot's [high-level multiplayer RPC documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html#remote-procedure-calls)
  defines reliable, unreliable and unreliable-ordered transfer modes and warns that
  ordered packets on one channel should have similar transfer characteristics.
- [`MultiplayerSynchronizer`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_multiplayersynchronizer.html)
  synchronizes configured properties but cannot synchronize `Object`/`Resource`-like
  values; [`MultiplayerSpawner`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_multiplayerspawner.html)
  handles authority-driven spawn replication. Both are useful high-level tools, but
  mixing them with explicit RPC writers for the same fields violates this project's
  one-writer contract.
- [`PackedByteArray`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_packedbytearray.html)
  and [`StreamPeerBuffer`](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_streampeerbuffer.html)
  provide fixed-endian primitive encoding without Variant dictionaries per row.
- Glenn Fiedler's [Snapshot Interpolation](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
  describes buffering snapshots to absorb jitter and short loss, then limited
  extrapolation/freeze for gaps. His
  [State Synchronization](https://gafferongames.com/post/state_synchronization/)
  compares complete state updates with priority/rate selection.
- Gabriel Gambetta's [Entity Interpolation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html)
  explains the deliberate presentation delay and trade-off between smoothness and
  latency for remote entities.
- RFC 8085, [UDP Usage Guidelines](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8085.html#section-3.2),
  recommends avoiding IP fragmentation because one lost fragment loses the whole
  datagram. The prototype keeps each application motion payload at or below 1,200
  bytes, but it is not an external-path MTU proof.
- The project's [ENet server bandwidth defect](../upstream/godot-enet-create-server-bandwidth.md)
  applies here. S11 reuses unchanged `S03Transport`, including
  `host.bandwidth_limit(0, 0)` immediately after server creation and before peer
  publication.

## Options considered

### MultiplayerSpawner plus MultiplayerSynchronizer

This is the least custom code for ordinary node properties and spawn/despawn. It is
attractive for small homogeneous scenes and editor-configured replication. It gives
less direct control over quantization, row batching, exact application bytes and
lifecycle dependency fences, and would require replacing the existing explicit
Replication writer field by field. It was not selected for M1 population motion.

### Packed complete snapshots plus reliable lifecycle — selected

A 16-byte row carries entity ID/generation/kind/phase, centimetre-quantized X/Z and
velocity, and quantized yaw. A 12-byte packet header supplies sequence, server tick
and bounded chunk metadata. Complete rows are independently useful after loss; each
entity has its own generation/tick fence. The costs are a project-owned codec and
repeating unchanged values. Those costs are acceptable given the measured margin
and substantially simpler recovery than delta chains.

### Delta snapshots and per-client interest management

Changed-field masks, baseline-relative deltas and view-based subscriptions can reduce
traffic. They also create per-client reference state, reliable reset/keyframe rules,
join/resync coupling, and relevance enter/leave lifecycle. A dropped reference can
make later deltas unusable. Reserve this option for a measured production limit.
Rate buckets and omission of reliably terminal entities capture the lowest-risk
savings without introducing delta ancestry.

## Prototype

The saved minimal scene is [S11 boot](../../tests/fixtures/s11/boot.tscn). It reuses
S03's unchanged ENet transport/session fixture files read-only in the isolated staged
project; `S03Transport` is the actual peer owner. S11 adds:

- [`S11SnapshotCodec`](../../tests/fixtures/s11/snapshot_codec.gd): fixed-width,
  big-endian movement chunks up to 1,200 application bytes, plus exact-size reliable
  lifecycle transactions;
- [`S11 proof`](../../tests/fixtures/s11/proof.gd): deterministic rail motion for all
  148 simultaneous entities, split subset publication, reliable baseline,
  death/wreck/despawn/spawn events, generation/terminal fences and remote
  interpolation/extrapolation telemetry;
- [`S11PopulationPolicy`](../../tests/fixtures/s11/population_policy.gd): pure bounded
  cap, all-player-view exclusion, reservation, commit/release and reset logic; and
- [`api_probe.gd`](../../tests/fixtures/s11/api_probe.gd): independent public-API
  checks for 148-row splitting, quantization, malformed rejection, lifecycle
  roundtrip, caps, view exclusion, clearance reservations and reset.

Every movement message contains one subset of at most 74 rows. Subsets alternate
every four host physics ticks, so every entity is revisited every eight ticks
(about 133 ms before transport loss), under the canonical 250 ms revisit bound.
The host emits 165 packets over 660 physics ticks. Reliable channel 1 owns baseline
and lifecycle; ordered-unreliable channel 3 owns motion. Motion never writes health,
seat, equipment or authoritative terminal outcomes.

The deterministic procedural population is intentional measurement data, not an
authored visible scene or AI substitute. It exercises codec cardinality and motion;
it does not claim actor physics, AI/query work or representative presentation cost.
The editor was unavailable, as declared by the lane brief. Text authoring was used
for the minimal allowed scene, then the staged scene was loaded/resaved with the
pinned engine and assigned its retained scene UID. Script UIDs came from pinned
headless import. No S03 file was edited.

[`run_s11.py`](../../tools/run_s11.py) stages an addon-free project and launches one
host and three clients as distinct processes. Each client has an independent seeded
UDP proxy route. Normal is 75 ms ±30 ms one-way and 2% independent loss. Adverse is
125 ms ±50 ms, 5% loss and a one-second bidirectional blackout. Proxy queues are
bounded to 1,024 entries and receive/delivery work to 128 entries per poll. Cleanup
stops only owned `Popen` children.

The proxy counts every endpoint datagram at ingress, including dropped traffic and
retransmissions, then adds 20-byte IPv4 and 8-byte UDP headers. Thus the result
includes ENet/RPC and IP/UDP overhead but excludes link-layer overhead. A join-wire
cut includes connection/admission traffic through baseline completion. Worst 10 s
windows are computed over timestamped aggregate host traffic and each client route.

## Results

Canonical command:

```sh
export PATH="$(dirname "$(mise -C C:/GameDev/git/fun-things which godot)"):$(dirname "$(mise -C C:/GameDev/git/fun-things which gdstyle)"):$PATH"
timeout 900 python tools/run_s11.py \
  --profiles normal adverse --repetitions 3 --clients 3 \
  --port 25360 --proxy-port 25361 --deadline 40 \
  --output C:/tmp/ft/lanes/s11-population-net/full-09
```

All six cases passed their codec, lifecycle, size, budget and process/log criteria.
Each case installed all 148 baseline entities on every client. All nine clients per
profile received `death`, `wreck`, `despawn`, `spawn` in reliable revision order;
stale cross-channel rows were rejected rather than recreating the despawned
generation. Maximum application motion payload was 1,196 bytes. The application
codec averaged 16.162 bytes per entity row including amortized packet headers.

Bandwidth below is KiB/s over the worst 10 s byte window. “Client” is the worst of
three clients in that repetition. Budgets are host out/in 256/128 and client in/out
96/48 KiB/s.

| Profile / metric | Three repetitions | Median | Worst | Budget result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Normal host out | 50.69, 58.20, 53.58 | 53.58 | 58.20 | pass |
| Normal host in | 0.577, 0.498, 0.570 | 0.570 | 0.577 | pass |
| Normal client in | 17.45, 19.53, 17.99 | 17.99 | 19.53 | pass |
| Normal client out | 0.198, 0.186, 0.194 | 0.194 | 0.198 | pass |
| Adverse host out | 58.50, 56.78, 58.34 | 58.34 | 58.50 | pass |
| Adverse host in | 0.338, 0.324, 0.324 | 0.324 | 0.338 | pass |
| Adverse client in | 19.83, 20.06, 20.06 | 20.06 | 20.06 | pass |
| Adverse client out | 0.117, 0.133, 0.118 | 0.118 | 0.133 | pass |

The packed baseline payload was 2,392 bytes/client. Worst join traffic including
handshake/ENet/IP/UDP overhead was 3,149 bytes normal and 3,269 bytes adverse, far
below 1 MiB. This baseline contains only the synthetic movement/lifecycle schema;
it does not estimate definitions, weapons, seats, durable journal or city content.
Client-out and host-in are transport acknowledgements/control only: this fixture has
no production held-input stream, so their low values are not an input-load estimate.

Timing values are **contended upper bounds**. Before cases, 7–11 Godot processes
were observed; during measurement the total was 10–15. Sampled CPU load reached 85%
before cases and 94% during measured starts; a few CIM samples returned 0% and are
not treated as idle evidence. One 74-row encode packet had: normal
median-of-repetition p95 797 µs, 4.468 ms worst sample; adverse median p95 1.324 ms,
55.158 ms worst sample. Decode had normal median p95 1.298 ms, 14.710 ms worst;
adverse median p95 1.570 ms, 6.752 ms worst. These are script-level timers around codec
calls, not full tick CPU, AI/physics, profiler-isolated time or quiet-device results.
A quiet rerun is required before budgeting production tick time.

The headless presentation proxy measured deviation from expected constant-velocity
step and bounded extrapolation, not pixels or subjective smoothness:

| Profile | client jitter p95 median / worst | extrapolated entity-frame median / worst |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Normal | 0.083 / 0.102 m | 11.69% / 21.75% |
| Adverse | 0.060 / 0.091 m | 19.53% / 39.46% |

The lower adverse jitter is not better continuity: more frames were extrapolated or
frozen, reducing measured step variation. The one-second blackout intentionally
forces extrapolation after the 200 ms extrapolation cap. Contention and accelerated/
irregular headless process pacing also affect these values. They demonstrate that
bandwidth headroom alone does not prove smooth remote presentation.

## Population owner and spawning policy

Production Population is the sole authority for NPC slots, replenishment, NPC life,
retention and reset. It asks CityData's shared clearance query for authored candidates
and uses Match's shared reservations; it does not invent a second collision formula.
The proposed policy is:

1. Keep global caps at 64 pedestrians and 32 live cars, with separate retained caps
   of 16 wrecks and 16 dead pedestrians. Occupied and parked cars still consume the
   32-live-car cap. Refuse replenishment before reserving if the relevant cap is full.
2. Schedule replenishment as bounded work: inspect at most ten authored candidates
   per population job per tick, carry the job to a later tick if none qualifies, and
   enforce a deadline/backoff rather than scanning the district in one callback.
3. Reject candidates visible to **any** admitted player's current authoritative view,
   including a reviewed hysteresis margin. A fixed 30 m circle exists only in the
   pure fixture test; production must use the accepted 42° camera/view geometry or a
   conservative CityData visibility-cell query. Occlusion must not be inferred from
   a client report.
4. Run CityData's kind-specific world/ground/static/live-body/wreck/reservation
   clearance with the final actor or vehicle envelope. Atomically reserve before
   adding a body, then commit or release in the same owner transaction. This reuses
   S06's clearance owner; the pure fixture tests reservation overlap only.
5. Never delete a live, occupied or active-chain entity to make replenishment room.
   Retained-cap eviction applies only after the owner-complete retention deadline and
   dependent chain/lifecycle work.
6. Reset stops producers, clears jobs/reservations/timers and old revision callbacks,
   then reconstructs the declared initial population under the new MatchRevision.
   Old lifecycle/movement cannot repopulate the reset match.

## What is proven and what is not

**Proven in this bounded synthetic fixture:** custom 148-row codec size and malformed
checks; complete all-entity refresh below 1,200 application bytes/message; actual
four-process ENet delivery through three independent proxies; reliable lifecycle
ordering separate from motion; generation/terminal movement fences; measured
IP/UDP-inclusive traffic below all four budgets in six contended cases; tiny synthetic
join baseline below 1 MiB; bounded proxy/process cleanup; pure cap/reservation/reset
logic.

**Not proven:** production schema/baseline/journal size; predecoder/native allocation
bounds; external path MTU or IP-fragment capture; player input traffic; actual AI,
physics, clearance or spawn visibility; actor/car/projectile motion extras; rendered
interpolation, animation, camera continuity or human smoothness; sustained ten-minute
load; quiet CPU cost; real combat/lifecycle/reset/join races; abusive traffic; LAN,
packaged Windows/Linux or any non-ENet transport. The deterministic rails do not
satisfy S07's representative-population requirement.

## Production requirements

1. Give Replication one versioned packed schema with exact decode bounds, centimetre
   range review for the final district, generation/life/control/collision dependencies
   and per-entity tick watermarks. No Synchronizer/Spawner may also write those fields.
2. Keep motion at or below 1,200 application bytes. Split only between complete rows,
   stagger subsets, and revisit every relevant entity within 250 ms before loss.
   Retain the ENet bandwidth workaround and throttle regression.
3. Send lifecycle/seat/death/wreck/spawn/despawn transactions reliably before dependent
   motion. Retain tombstones and newest bounded future row per entity; request canonical
   RESYNC on dependency gap/overflow rather than recreating from motion.
4. Implement the full immutable baseline and durable journal/handoff contract. Measure
   definitions, players, NPCs, live cars, retained bodies, active rockets, weapon/seat/
   collision state, chunk framing and actual transport overhead against 1 MiB.
5. Start with replicate-all. Add explicit rate buckets without relevance lifecycle:
   measure roughly 15–20 Hz near/fast actors, 7.5–10 Hz ordinary movers and 4 Hz idle
   refresh while preserving the 250 ms bound. Exact rates/distances are tuning inputs,
   not accepted values from this spike. Re-measure actual row extras first.
6. Validate a 200–300 ms remote interpolation buffer, limited extrapolation then freeze,
   and correction smoothing in drawable normal/adverse cases. Record entity-frame
   extrapolation, correction distance, visual hitches and lifecycle snap behavior.
7. Implement Population's bounded authored-candidate/view/CityData-clearance policy,
   cap/retention accounting, reservations, backoff and reset. Unit-test all-player view
   exclusion, same-tick races, blocked districts, cap pressure and stale callbacks.
8. Re-run at full production caps for at least ten minutes with three clients, held
   input, real AI/physics/chains and join during mutation. Measure quiet and contended
   CPU, full tick p95/p99, queues and worst 10 s traffic; retain failed runs.

## Open owner question

During an adverse one-second delivery interruption, should remote actors extrapolate
longer (risking larger corrections) or freeze after a short bound (visible pause but
no invented travel)? The fixture uses 200 ms then freezes. This needs a drawable
comparison and owner feel decision; it does not block the packed replication direction.
