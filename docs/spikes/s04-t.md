# S04-T — predicted foot/car transition fixture

8 October 2026. This bounded two-process fixture executes the P0-readiness G2 seat-transfer
cases on Windows with the pinned Godot engine. It is technical transition evidence, not a
production interaction system, physical-input test, handling-feel acceptance, Linux/export
result, or full-world rollback proof.

The Godot editor was not running and MCP editor tools were unavailable. The task explicitly
required a new fixture, so the small saved test scene and scripts were authored directly and
then imported with the pinned engine. No accepted S03-R, S04, or S09 source was changed.

## Implemented boundary

The saved fixture instances the accepted S03 predicted actor and 120-frame
`S03PredictionHistory`, plus the accepted S04 `S04Kinematic` car and `S04DriveRules`. It
contains one player, one parked car, and one deterministically host-driven traffic car. The
traffic controller is fixture-only and read-only with respect to S09; it submits a complete
drive command through the shared S04 rule rather than adding a second handling formula.

One real remote client and one host-scripted claimant submit the first parked-car claim for
the same accepted host tick. Authority sorts by `(accepted_tick, participant_id,
action_sequence)`, grants the scripted lower participant, and returns `SEAT_OCCUPIED` to
the remote client. The RPC boundary derives the real claimant from
`multiplayer.get_remote_sender_id()`; no participant identity is accepted over the wire.
The scripted claim is released before the client's later successful parked-car claim.

The client predicts an entry immediately. A reliable host transition either commits the
selected car or restores the authoritative foot pose after rejection. Every foot↔car commit
clears both replay domains before opening the new one, changes collision/simulation ownership,
and flips the saved camera and HUD owner together. Ongoing snapshots acknowledge the newest
host-simulated input tick; only the active S03/S04 history is replayed. No car command has a
fire field and no weapon path is reachable while seated.

Authority rejects exit at speed **>= 0.5 m/s** with `EXIT_MOVING`. The first stopped exit has
both fixed candidates blocked and returns `EXIT_BLOCKED` without changing seat, controller,
or replay ownership. The next stopped exit succeeds at the authored left offset. Entering the
moving traffic car stops its fixture AI before player controls begin. When the client closes
its real ENet peer while driving that car, authority releases the seat immediately, substitutes
neutral S04 input, and coasts the surviving car to rest; it does not resume AI. The abandoned
car remains parked.

The proxy is seeded and bounded. “Normal” is 75 ms +/-30 ms one-way with 2% random datagram
loss. “Adverse” is 125 ms +/-50 ms one-way with 5% loss and a 500 ms blackout. Both are real
separate-process ENet runs. Every graphical process is explicitly capped at 60 FPS through
`tools/window_safety.py`; source/invocation identity uses `tools/measurement_identity.py`.

## Selected measurements

Selected evidence is bound to commit `c1902a6ae6b314d00758be0e623c5860a482dabf`, a clean tree,
and Godot `4.8.dev7.official.c971f93e7`. The camera/actor discontinuity is the distance between
the controlled presentation focus immediately before and after the reliable transition. It
is a technical saved-scene frame metric, not display scanout or subjective visual acceptance.
The retained PNGs show the rejected race and accepted traffic entry in each profile.

| Profile | Predicted entry correction / camera jump | Accepted parked | Accepted traffic | Accepted time-to-control p95 | Action RTT p95 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Normal | 2.592 m rejected | 0.176 m | 1.000 m | 18 ms | 201 ms |
| Adverse | 2.982 m rejected | 0.363 m | 0.333 m | 17 ms | 367 ms |

The successful stopped-exit focus jump was 1.522 m normal and 1.500 m adverse, matching the
authored exit offset. Moving and blocked rejections produced no ownership or camera jump.
All four actual transfer/rejection boundaries (`race_entry`, `parked_entry`, successful exit,
and `traffic_entry`) reported both histories empty at installation. Camera and HUD owner values
matched on all six verdicts. Both runs independently observed the full verdict sequence,
the authority-granted race, traffic AI release, real disconnect, displacement under neutral
coast (49.768 m normal / 49.877 m adverse from an approximately 20 m/s disconnect), and final
speed below 0.01 m/s. All retained process exits were zero and scoped logs contained no engine
or script diagnostics.

Time-to-control measures request-to-first predicted car physics frame, so it remains local and
low under impairment. It does not claim that authority accepted the request in 18 ms. The
rejected prediction correction is intentionally reported rather than hidden: this minimal
fixture switches camera focus at prediction time, then returns to a foot actor that continued
on authority while reliable denial was in flight.

## Evidence and reproduction

Selected source-bound summaries, raw host/client telemetry, proxy delivery logs, and four PNGs
are under [`s04-t-evidence/`](s04-t-evidence/). No scratch project is committed.

```sh
python tools/s04_t/run.py --godot C:/path/to/godot.exe \
  --profiles normal adverse --windowed \
  --output C:/tmp/ft/lanes/s04-t/selected
```

The output directory must be fresh and outside every checkout. The runner stages only the
fixture dependencies into an addon-free project, imports that copy, starts one host and one
client, and stops only its own `Popen` children. The independent telemetry counterexamples are
included in normal Python test discovery.

## Disposition and production requirements

The bounded G2 mechanism passes its executable criteria: authoritative race ordering,
`EXIT_MOVING`, blocked-exit retention, traffic-controller release, disconnect coast, and clean
predicted-body handoff all execute over real ENet under both profiles. The experiment also
shows that immediate predicted camera reassignment is not production-ready: a rejected entry
created a 2.6–3.0 m correction, and accepted moving-traffic entry varied up to 1.0 m.

Production should keep the reliable transition as the only durable installer, preserve camera
world framing across predicted owner changes, and define a snap/smooth threshold for rejected
entry separately from physics correction. It must integrate real authored clearance queries,
range/eligibility checks, production S09 traffic control, entity generations and control
revisions, destruction/death/reset races, moving-body collision history, and the production
Match/HUD rig. It also needs physical controls, subjective camera/handling review, multi-client
rather than scripted claimants, Linux, and exported-build checks.

Suggested `docs/plans/task-requirements.md` replacement (not edited per lane brief): mark S04-T
complete with links to this record and evidence, while retaining production camera smoothing,
clearance, full lifecycle races, and real S09/Match integration as M1 work.
