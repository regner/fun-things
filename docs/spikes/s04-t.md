## Implemented boundary

The saved fixture instances the accepted S03 predicted actor, `S03PredictionHistory`, and
`S03InputFrameQueue`, plus the accepted S04 `S04Kinematic` car and `S04DriveRules`. It contains
one player, one parked car, and one deterministically host-driven traffic car. The fixture AI
submits complete drive commands through the shared S04 rule and does not change S09.

One real remote client and one host-scripted claimant submit the first parked-car claim for
the same accepted host tick. Authority sorts by `(accepted_tick, participant_id,
action_sequence)`, grants the scripted lower participant, and returns `SEAT_OCCUPIED` to the
remote client. The RPC boundary derives the real claimant from
`multiplayer.get_remote_sender_id()`; no participant identity is accepted over the wire. The
scripted claim is released before the client's later successful parked-car claim.

The client predicts entry immediately. A reliable host transition either commits the selected
car or restores the authoritative foot pose after rejection. Every foot↔car commit clears both
replay domains before opening the new one, changes collision/simulation ownership, resets held
sequence numbering for the new control revision, and flips the saved camera and HUD owner
together. No car command has a fire field and no weapon path is reachable while seated.

Authority rejects exit at speed **>= 0.5 m/s** with `EXIT_MOVING`. The first stopped request is
intentionally forced to return `EXIT_BLOCKED`; this proves atomic retention after that verdict,
not a geometric clearance query. The seat, controller, and prediction domain remain unchanged.
The next stopped exit succeeds at the authored left offset. Entering the moving traffic car
stops fixture AI before player controls begin. When the client closes its real ENet peer while
driving that car, authority releases the seat immediately, substitutes neutral S04 input, and
coasts the surviving car to rest without resuming AI.

## Network admission and prediction semantics

Every fixture RPC accepts one validated `Variant` envelope. Host-bound hydration, held input,
and action requests require exact fields and types, sender admission, control revision, finite
numbers, allowed enums/ranges, and actual `var_to_bytes` size limits before mutation.
Client-bound baselines, snapshots, and reliable transitions validate exact nested fields,
finite bounded poses, revisions, failure enums, and serialized extent before installation.
Only one hydration is admitted per native peer.

Held input uses the accepted S03 authority queue for both foot and car revisions:

- at most 1200 serialized bytes and three consecutive frames per envelope
- 60 messages/s with burst eight
- eight pending frames, separately from the 120-sequence freshness window
- one consumed frame per host tick
- oldest frame within three sequence/input ticks of newest accepted, with older work explicitly
  superseded into the acknowledgement watermark
- receipt age over 250 ms supersedes pending frames and simulates neutral input
- sequence, watermark, queue, and retained client history reset at each host control revision

The client sends three-frame redundancy and varying foot/car commands. Host telemetry records
each consumed/superseded watermark, revision, queue depth, receipt age, expiry, and command
sample. Redundancy is cleared whenever speculative mode changes, but packet sequence remains
monotonic until authority advances the control revision. In both selected runs, revision-one
authority input continued beyond the rejected race entry (through sequence 38 normal and 47
adverse); no `INPUT_QUEUE` rejection remained. All four observed revisions restarted within
the eight-frame authority window, and both predicted histories cleared at transfer before
replay began in the new domain.

Reliable actions are limited to 4096 bytes, 16 requests/s with burst 32, 16 queued requests,
four processed per participant per host tick, a 64-result deduplication cache, and sequence at
most 64 ahead. Duplicate queued requests do no work; cached duplicates reuse their result.
`boundary_probe.gd` exercises malformed/extra fields, NaN/INF, out-of-range controls, oversized
serialization, held sequence-ahead/capacity, fresh control-revision restart, repeated hydration,
action deduplication, queue flood, token-bucket flood, per-tick work, cache retirement, and
malformed client-bound state. Both selected staged probes print `S04-T BOUNDARY PASS` without
engine diagnostics.

## Disposition and production requirements

The corrected bounded G2 mechanism passes its executable criteria: authoritative race ordering,
`EXIT_MOVING`, forced `EXIT_BLOCKED` retention, traffic-controller release, disconnect coast,
validated and work-bounded RPC admission, accepted S03 queue/watermark/expiry semantics for
both predicted domains, control-revision reset, and clean predicted-body handoff all execute
under normal and the full accepted adverse profile.

Production must preserve camera world framing across predicted owner changes and define a
snap/smooth threshold for rejected and moving-traffic entry separately from physics correction.
It must replace the forced blocked verdict with authored-candidate clearance queries, and add
range/eligibility checks, production S09 traffic control, durable entity generations, full
transition/result separation, destruction/death/reset races, moving-body collision history,
and the production Match/HUD rig. It also needs physical controls, subjective camera/handling
review, real multi-client rather than scripted claimants, Linux, and exported-build checks.
